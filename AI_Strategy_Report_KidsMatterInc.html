Section 01

Who is Kids Matter Inc.?

Before exploring AI tools, we grounded every recommendation in what KMI actually does. The goal is simple: give staff more time for the families they serve.

"Because every child needs someone in their corner."

Founded in 2000, Kids Matter Inc. is a Milwaukee-based nonprofit that advocates for abused, neglected, and at-risk children across Wisconsin. They serve kinship caregivers, foster families, trauma survivors, youth aging out of the system, and families navigating child crime — all services offered completely free of charge. They hold a 4-star Charity Navigator rating and are recognized as a Top-Rated nonprofit by Great Nonprofits.

CASA — Court Appointed Special Advocates

KMI manages Milwaukee County's entire CASA program. Trained volunteers are appointed by judges to independently investigate abuse and neglect cases and write court reports that directly shape outcomes for children — often the first report a judge reads.

🏥

Fostering Healing

A multidisciplinary team — therapists, legal advocates, social workers, and resource specialists — supports children and kinship caregivers through trauma-focused therapy (TF-CBT), legal representation, school placement, and crisis counseling.

📋

Legal Navigation for Families

KMI guides kinship caregivers — grandparents, aunts, uncles, siblings, family friends — through guardianship filings, power of attorney, foster care processes, and ICWA compliance in plain language.

🧒

Child Safety Education

An extensive public library covering types of abuse, mandatory reporting obligations, trafficking awareness, trauma recovery, and victim rights — serving the broader Milwaukee community and beyond.

🎂

Birthday Buddy Program

Foster and kinship youth receive birthday bags — a small but deeply human program ensuring every child in the system feels recognized and celebrated on their special day.

🎓

Youth Transition Support

For teens aging out of foster care: guidance on housing, postsecondary education, college funding, health care power of attorney, and navigating independent adult life — a population often left behind by the system.

Section 04

How to Talk to an AI — A Beginner's Guide

Using Claude or ChatGPT is like giving instructions to a brilliant new volunteer who just walked in the door. They don't know your organization, your tone, or your clients — but brief them well, and they do extraordinary work. Here's exactly how.

The Golden Rule

You are not using a search engine — you are having a conversation with a collaborator. The more context you give about who you are, what you need, and what the output is for, the better the result. One extra sentence of context can transform a generic response into something that feels written specifically for Kids Matter Inc.

1

Always tell Claude who you are and what you're trying to accomplish

Don't ask a standalone question. Start by explaining your role, your organization, and what the output will be used for. This single habit is the biggest lever beginner users can pull.

❌ Too vague
Write a social media post about foster care.
✅ Strong prompt
You are helping Kids Matter Inc., a Milwaukee nonprofit supporting kinship and foster families. Our tone is warm, hopeful, and community-focused. Write a Facebook post for National Foster Care Month celebrating our 100+ CASA volunteers. Include a call to volunteer. Under 200 words, end with 3 hashtags.
2

Specify the format and length you want

Claude will choose a length that seems reasonable — unless you specify otherwise. Always say how long, in what format, and how many options you want.

Draft a grant narrative for the CASA program. - Format: 3 paragraphs, no bullet points - Tone: professional but accessible, not overly formal - Length: 400–500 words - Focus on: volunteer impact, children served, and outcomes - Use [X] as a placeholder wherever I need to fill in numbers
3

Set up a "Claude Project" — your organization's permanent context

In claude.ai, a Project is a workspace where Claude always remembers who you are before every conversation. Think of it as a staff handbook that Claude reads automatically each time you open a chat.

HOW TO SET UP YOUR PROJECT: 1. Go to claude.ai → click "Projects" in the left sidebar 2. Click "New Project" → name it "Kids Matter Inc." 3. In the Project Instructions box, paste something like: --- You are an AI assistant for Kids Matter Inc., a nonprofit in Milwaukee, WI that supports kinship and foster families. Our programs: CASA volunteer advocacy, Fostering Healing (trauma therapy + legal help), Birthday Buddy Program, and legal navigation for families. Our tone: warm, hopeful, accessible. Avoid jargon. Audience varies: families in crisis, judges, funders, and the general public. Always handle child trauma topics with care and sensitivity. --- 4. Every conversation in this Project starts with that context automatically — you never have to re-explain.
4

Upload documents directly — don't retype what already exists

Claude can read PDFs, Word documents, and spreadsheets. Instead of describing a document, simply attach it and ask Claude to work with it. This is one of the most powerful but underused features.

Examples of what you can upload and ask Claude to do: 📄 Upload last year's annual report → "Write a 2-paragraph funder update using the outcome data in this report. Focus on children served and volunteer hours." 📊 Upload a spreadsheet of volunteer hours → "Summarize these hours by month and write 3 sentences highlighting our most impactful quarter." 📝 Upload an existing grant application → "Adapt this narrative for a new funder focused on trauma-informed care. Adjust references from Milwaukee County to statewide impact."
5

Treat the first response as a draft — ask for revisions freely

The first response is never the last word. Iteration is normal and expected. Don't accept a result that doesn't feel right — just tell Claude what to change in plain English.

After getting a draft, try: "Make this shorter and more emotional — less formal." "The second paragraph doesn't sound like us. Rewrite it to feel warmer and more personal." "Add a fictional composite story of a child helped by a CASA volunteer." "Give me 3 different versions of the opening line." "This is too long for an email — condense to 150 words."
6

Build a shared "Prompt Library" — reusable recipes for your whole team

Once a prompt works well, save it in a shared Google Doc. Over time this becomes your team's AI toolbox — anyone can copy, paste, and get great results immediately, without needing to be an AI expert.

SAMPLE PROMPT LIBRARY ENTRIES: ── COURT REPORT DRAFT ── You are helping a CASA volunteer at Kids Matter Inc. write a court report for Milwaukee County Children's Court. Organize the notes below into: Background, Current Situation, Child's Expressed Wishes, Volunteer Observations, and Recommendations. Formal but readable language. Interview notes: [paste notes here] ── DONOR THANK YOU EMAIL ── Write a warm, 3-sentence thank you email to a donor who just gave $250 to Kids Matter Inc. Reference that their gift supports CASA volunteers who advocate for children in foster care. Sign off: "The Kids Matter Team." ── FACEBOOK — VOLUNTEER SPOTLIGHT ── Write a 150-word Facebook post spotlighting a CASA volunteer. Details: [paste here]. Call to action to volunteer. Warm, community tone. End with #KidsMatter #CASA #Milwaukee
Section 05

Privacy, Legality & AI — What You Need to Know

This is the most important section in the report. Before any staff member uses an AI tool with real client or organizational information, they should understand exactly what happens to that data, what the law says, and where the clear lines are. These concerns are legitimate and deserve a thorough, honest answer.

⚠️ The Short Answer to "Is It Safe?"

It depends entirely on what information you share and which tool you use. AI tools like Claude and ChatGPT are powerful allies for drafting, writing, and organizing internal work. But they are not automatically HIPAA-compliant, and real client names, case details, and identifying information should never be entered into a standard consumer account. This section tells you exactly where the lines are — and how to stay on the right side of them.

What Actually Happens to Information You Share with an AI?

When you type something into Claude or ChatGPT, it travels to the company's servers, gets processed, and a response is generated. What happens after that varies significantly by provider and account type — which is why it matters which tool you use and how you use it.

🤖 Standard Consumer Accounts (Free & Basic Paid Plans)

On a standard personal account at claude.ai or chat.openai.com:

  • Your conversations may be reviewed by human staff for safety and quality purposes
  • Your inputs may be used to improve the model's future training, depending on your settings
  • Data is stored on the provider's servers for a period of time
  • These accounts are NOT appropriate for real client names, case numbers, or sensitive personal information

🔒 Enterprise & API Accounts

On Anthropic's Claude for Work (Teams/Enterprise) or OpenAI's Enterprise plan:

  • Your conversations are not used for model training by default
  • Human review of conversations is prohibited or heavily restricted
  • Data retention policies are controlled and can be customized
  • These plans are designed for organizational use with stronger privacy protections
  • Neither is automatically HIPAA-compliant without a Business Associate Agreement (BAA)

HIPAA and Child Welfare Privacy Laws — What Applies to KMI?

Kids Matter Inc. handles sensitive health and child welfare information daily. Multiple laws govern how that information must be protected:

⚕️ HIPAA

Applies to any "protected health information" (PHI) — this includes therapy session details, mental health diagnoses, treatment plans, and medical information for children in your care. Using a standard AI tool with PHI is a HIPAA violation. An enterprise AI tool with a signed Business Associate Agreement (BAA) can be compliant.

👶 FERPA

Applies to student education records. If KMI receives school records as part of a CASA investigation or educational advocacy, those records are covered. Do not paste a child's IEP, disciplinary records, or school assessment into a standard AI tool.

⚖️ Wisconsin Child Welfare Laws

Wisconsin Statute Chapter 938 and DCF rules protect the confidentiality of children in out-of-home care. Case information, court records, and child welfare data may not be shared outside authorized channels — which generally does not include third-party AI platforms without explicit data agreements.

Can We Use AI to Transcribe Internal Meetings?

🎙️ Meeting Transcription: Proceed with Real Caution

This is the area where organizations most commonly make mistakes that create legal and ethical risk. Here is the full picture — the concerns are real, but there are safe ways to use transcription tools.

⚠️ Legal Concerns — Wisconsin Wiretapping Law

Wisconsin is a one-party consent state for recording — meaning you generally only need one person in the conversation to consent to recording. However:

  • All participants should be notified at the start of the call that it is being recorded or transcribed, even if only one-party consent is technically required
  • Recording a meeting where a client or family member is present requires their explicit informed consent — especially for minors
  • For meetings involving attorneys, court officials, or sensitive case discussions, consult KMI's legal counsel before using transcription
  • Recordings stored by a third-party AI company (like Otter.ai or Fireflies.ai) are governed by that company's data policies — those records may be stored, reviewed, or used in ways KMI cannot control

🔐 What to Do Instead — Safer Alternatives

  • Internal admin meetings only: Transcription of purely internal staff meetings (budgets, scheduling, general coordination) carries minimal risk if all staff are notified and the tool has strong privacy policies
  • Local-only transcription: Tools like Whisper (offline) run entirely on your own computer — no data ever leaves. Safest for sensitive content
  • Summarize notes, don't transcribe: Write rough bullet-point notes during a meeting, then ask Claude to organize and expand them into meeting minutes — without ever recording audio or sharing a full transcript
  • Never transcribe client or family sessions without a specific signed authorization from the client, legal guardian, and KMI's attorney. Therapy sessions have additional protections under Wisconsin privilege laws
  • If using a cloud transcription tool for internal meetings, review that tool's BAA availability and data retention policies first

The Three Zones of AI Use at KMI

✅

Green Zone — Use Freely

Grant writing, newsletters, social media, donor emails, volunteer training Q&A, meeting minutes from internal admin meetings (no client info), resource research, anonymized session note drafts using only case codes.

⚠️

Yellow Zone — Proceed with Care

Summarizing internal staff meetings (notify all participants, use a tool with strong privacy terms), creating composite/fictional case examples for training materials, drafting forms that could later be used with client names.

🚫

Red Zone — Do Not Use Without Legal Review

Any real client names, case numbers, or child identifiers. Actual therapy session notes with PHI. Recordings of any session involving a client or family member. Court records or DCF documentation. School records for children in care.

Practical Steps KMI Can Take Right Now

📋 Create a Simple AI Use Policy

  • One-page internal document that defines Green/Yellow/Red zone uses
  • Specifies which AI tools are approved (and at what plan level)
  • Requires anonymization before any case-adjacent information is used
  • Sets a review schedule (update annually as AI laws evolve)
  • Does not require legal expertise to create — Claude can draft a first version from this document

🔑 Consider an Enterprise Account

  • Claude for Work (Teams plan) and ChatGPT Enterprise both offer stronger data protections — conversations are not used for training, human review is restricted
  • Costs are modest for a small organization (~$25–30/user/month)
  • For anything touching health data, request a Business Associate Agreement (BAA) from the provider before proceeding
  • Anthropic offers BAAs for enterprise customers — ask your account representative

🧠 Train All Staff Before They Start

  • A 30-minute staff session covering this Privacy section is enough to set safe habits from the beginning
  • The biggest risk isn't that AI is dangerous — it's that well-meaning staff accidentally share more than they should because no one told them not to
  • Post the three-zone Green/Yellow/Red guide in a shared team folder for easy reference
  • Designate one staff member as the AI point of contact for questions

⚖️ Consult Legal Counsel on One Question

  • Ask KMI's attorney to review whether your specific use of AI for session note drafts (using case codes only, no names) requires a BAA or falls under existing data handling policies
  • This is a one-time consultation, not an ongoing burden
  • The field is evolving — Wisconsin and federal guidance on AI in healthcare settings is actively being developed as of 2025–2026
  • Being proactive now protects the organization from liability later

✅ The Bottom Line

AI is a genuine, powerful tool for organizations like Kids Matter Inc. — and the privacy concerns, while real, are manageable with clear guidelines. The risk is not from AI itself, but from using it without a framework. The organizations that benefit most from AI are the ones that set simple, clear rules early and train their team once. Everything in the Green Zone can start today. The Yellow and Red Zones simply require a bit more care — and most of KMI's highest-value AI uses fall squarely in the Green Zone.
